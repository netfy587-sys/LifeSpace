Where flavour comes from

Three things that shape a cuisine

No dish exists in isolation — it's shaped by what grows nearby, what's been traded in, and what's been carried forward from generation to generation.

Local geography

Rice paddies, wheat fields, coastlines, and mountains all decide what a region can grow or catch — and that becomes the backbone of its cooking.

Trade & migration

Spices, chillies, and even entire crops moved across oceans through trade and migration, quietly reshaping cuisines far from where those ingredients began.

Family & ritual

Recipes are often taught, not written down — passed between generations through festivals, Sunday meals, and the daily rhythm of a household kitchen.

A tour of the table

Signature dishes, region by region

A small, far-from-complete sampling of dishes that define how each region eats.

Ramen

East Asia — Japan

A noodle soup built on a slow-simmered broth — often pork bone, chicken, or miso-based — topped with sliced pork, soft-boiled egg, and seaweed. Though it traces back to Chinese wheat noodles, ramen became a distinctly Japanese comfort food over the 20th century.

Paella

Southern Europe — Spain

A saffron-coloured rice dish from Valencia, traditionally cooked over an open flame in a wide, shallow pan, mixing rice with vegetables, seafood, or meat depending on the region and season.

Tacos al pastor

The Americas — Mexico

Marinated pork, cooked on a vertical spit, sliced thin, and served on a small corn tortilla with pineapple, onion, and cilantro — a dish that itself blends Mexican tradition with techniques brought by Lebanese immigrants in the 20th century.

Butter chicken

South Asia — India

Tandoor-roasted chicken simmered in a rich tomato and butter gravy, invented in Delhi in the 1950s as a way to use up leftover tandoori chicken by cooking it in a creamy sauce.

Jollof rice

West Africa

A one-pot rice dish cooked in a tomato and pepper base, claimed with great pride by several West African nations, each with their own version and their own strong opinion on whose is best.

Hummus & mezze

The Middle East

A spread of small dishes — hummus, baba ghanoush, tabbouleh, and flatbread — built around sharing, and often served as the opening act of a much longer meal.

The craft behind the plate

Cooking techniques from around the world

Many techniques developed independently in different regions, often solving the same problem — preserving food, tenderising it, or drawing out flavour — in very different ways.

Fermentation

Used in kimchi, sourdough bread, miso, and yoghurt, fermentation relies on bacteria or yeast to preserve food and build deep, complex flavour over time.

Tandoor roasting

A clay oven that reaches extremely high temperatures, traditionally used across South and Central Asia to roast meat and bake flatbreads like naan almost instantly.

Stir-frying

A quick, high-heat technique from Chinese cooking that cooks ingredients fast in a wok, keeping vegetables crisp and flavours bright.

Slow braising

Common in French and many other cuisines, braising cooks tougher cuts of meat slowly in liquid, breaking them down into something tender over several hours.

From field to plate

A short history of food

How eating went from foraging to farming to a genuinely global exchange of ingredients.

c. 10,000 BCE

The start of agriculture

Communities in the Fertile Crescent began deliberately planting and harvesting crops, moving away from a purely hunter-gatherer way of life.

c. 1st century BCE – 1400s CE

The spice trade

Routes connecting Asia, the Middle East, and Europe carried pepper, cinnamon, and cloves across continents, making spices some of the most valuable goods in the ancient world.

1492 onward

The Columbian Exchange

Contact between the Americas and Europe moved tomatoes, potatoes, and chillies eastward, and wheat and livestock westward — reshaping cuisines on both sides of the Atlantic.

1800s

Canning and food preservation

New preservation methods let food travel further and last longer, changing both military logistics and everyday home cooking.

1950s

The rise of fast food

Standardised, quickly prepared meals scaled into global chains, changing how — and how often — many people eat outside the home.

Today

A genuinely global kitchen

Fusion cooking, food delivery apps, and easy access to ingredients from anywhere have made most home kitchens more globally connected than ever before.

Names in the kitchen

A few figures who shaped how we cook

French cuisine

Auguste Escoffier

French chef who modernised and organised classical French cooking, and whose kitchen brigade system is still used in professional kitchens today.

Home cooking

Julia Child

American chef and author who introduced French cooking to home cooks in the United States through her books and television programmes.

Japanese cuisine

Jiro Ono

Japanese sushi chef whose decades of dedication to a single craft made him one of the most respected figures in Japanese culinary tradition.

Indian cuisine

Tarla Dalal

Indian cookbook author whose vegetarian recipes and cooking shows shaped how millions of Indian households approached everyday cooking.

Small bites

Interesting facts about food

Honey found in ancient Egyptian tombs, thousands of years old, has been found still perfectly edible.

Chilli peppers weren't part of Indian, Thai, or Korean cooking until they arrived from the Americas after 1492.

Saffron is the world's most expensive spice by weight, since each flower yields only three tiny threads.

Ketchup started out in China as a fermented fish sauce, long before tomatoes were ever added to it.

A single vanilla orchid flower must be hand-pollinated within hours of blooming, which is part of why real vanilla is so costly.

Pizza Margherita is said to have been named after Queen Margherita of Italy in 1889, its colours matching the Italian flag.

The world's hottest chilli peppers, like the Carolina Reaper, are measured on a scale originally developed in 1912.

Rice feeds more people worldwide than any other single crop, forming a staple food for over half the global population.

Kitchen vocabulary

A short glossary of cooking terms

Umami
Often called the "fifth taste," a savoury flavour found in ingredients like soy sauce, mushrooms, and aged cheese.
Mise en place
A French term meaning "everything in its place" — preparing and organising ingredients before cooking begins.
Deglaze
Adding liquid to a hot pan to lift browned bits of food stuck to the bottom, usually to build a sauce.
Marinate
Soaking food, usually meat, in a seasoned liquid before cooking to add flavour or tenderness.
Al dente
An Italian term describing pasta or rice cooked until just firm to the bite, not soft all the way through.
Reduction
A sauce or liquid simmered until some of it evaporates, concentrating its flavour.
